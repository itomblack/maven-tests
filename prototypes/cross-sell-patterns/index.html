What's actually happening during perimenopause

Perimenopause begins when your ovaries gradually produce less estrogen. For most women, this starts in the mid-40s — but it can begin as early as the late 30s.

The transition isn't a single event. It's a process that typically spans 4 to 8 years, during which hormone levels fluctuate unpredictably. This is why symptoms can feel so inconsistent — great one week, exhausting the next.

Common experiences during this time include irregular periods, sleep disruption, hot flashes, brain fog, mood changes, and shifts in libido. Many women describe feeling "not like themselves" without being able to point to a single cause.

The average woman spends a third of her life in post-menopause. How you navigate the transition matters.
